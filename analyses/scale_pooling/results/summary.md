# Pooling comparison: reverse-keyed items in scale vectors

Generated by `run_pooling_comparison.py`. Pooling functions live in `scale_pooling.py` (numpy only).

Variants: A `plain`, B `documented`, C `aligned` (plus `aligned_loo`, the leave-one-out version of C, an extra), D `hyperplane_old`, E `positive_only`, `F_composite` (composite correlation from the cosine item matrix with documented keying), `F_paper` (the paper's stored scale-level synthetic r). A-E are cosines between pooled vectors.

Files in this folder:

- `accuracy_metrics.csv`: accuracy table per dataset and variant
- `retrieval_metrics.csv`, `retrieval_per_query.csv`: retrieval means with bootstrap CIs, and per-query values
- `scale_pair_predictions.csv`: every scale pair with the empirical r and all predictions
- `aligned_items.csv`: every item-scale membership with keying, C and D diagnostics and empirical evidence; `aligned_sign_flips.csv` is the subset C flipped
- `hyperplane_scales.csv`: per-scale D diagnostics
- `paper_reproduction.csv`: stored vs re-computed empirical and synthetic scale r
- `dataset_info.csv`: sizes, embedding norms, keying source

## Dataset: pilot_holdout

493 respondents, 418 items, 113 scales, 6245 scale pairs (parent-facet pairs excluded, as in the paper). Item-scale memberships: 739, of which 261 reverse-keyed.

Keying source: derived: hand-coded key of the first item per scale (scales.keyed) + sign of the empirical correlation of every other item with that first item (global_functions.R::find_reverse_items_by_first_item).

Checks: stored embeddings have norms in [1.000000, 1.000000]. Reproduction of the paper's empirical scale r: Pearson 1.00000, max abs diff 3.6e-15. Reproduction of the paper's synthetic scale r (F_paper): Pearson 1.00000, max abs diff 1.7e-15. B (documented cosine) vs F_paper: Pearson 1.00000, max abs diff 1.7e-04. D vs E on scales where every reverse item was reflected: max abs vector diff 5.6e-17; max abs cosine diff over all pairs 0.492.

### Item-level sign geometry (context for C and D)

Mean raw cosine of each item with the other items of its scale (others signed by documentation): for the 261 documented-reverse items it is negative in 93% of cases (median -0.191); for the 478 positive items it is positive in 95% (median 0.269). Empirically the reverse items' raw correlation is negative in 100% of cases. So the model usually gets the direction of reverse items right, but with much weaker magnitude than for positive items, which is why D's 'cosine with the positive centroid < 0' test often fails.

### Variant C: items whose aligned sign disagrees with documentation

13 of 739 item-scale memberships changed sign (7 documented-reverse items became positive, 6 documented-positive items became reverse). 0 scales did not converge within 10 iterations. `cos_to_centroid` is the cosine of the documented-signed item with the final centroid. `mean_emp_r_raw` / `mean_cos_raw` are the mean empirical correlation / mean cosine of the RAW item with the other items of the scale (those signed by documentation): positive = the raw item runs with the construct. If `documented_reverse` is True and `mean_emp_r_raw` is negative, the documentation is right and the model has the sign wrong; if `mean_emp_r_raw` agrees with the model instead, the documentation is the likelier error.

Verdict by empirical evidence: 10 of 13 flips contradict the empirical keying (model sign errors), 3 agree with it (the documented keying is the likelier error).

### Variant D: reverse items not reflected

Of the remaining 61 mixed scales, 26 have at least one reverse item that was NOT reflected because its cosine with the positive centroid was already >= 0. Such an item is either a model sign error (the model already places it with the positive items) or a documentation error; see `aligned_items.csv` (columns hyperplane_reflected, mean_emp_r_raw_vs_scale) for the evidence.

At item level, 59 reverse items were not reflected. Their raw empirical correlation with the rest of the scale is negative for 58 (documentation right, model places the item on the wrong side) and positive for 1 (documented keying likely wrong). Median cosine with the positive centroid: 0.152.
